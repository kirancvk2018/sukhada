    Build the correct flow, step by step

    Pick a problem state from the dropdown above. A blank process skeleton will appear with the trigger, decision, and closure fixed in place — your job is to place the missing steps correctly.

    01

    Drag each card from the tray into the slot where it belongs — or tap a card, then tap a slot.

    02

    The tray includes distractor steps pulled from other scenarios. Not every card belongs.

    03

    Order matters in the pre-checks and the resolve path. In the escalate / vendor / approval branch the order is not graded — only that the right steps are there.

    04

    Submit when ready. Each slot is marked correct or incorrect, and your score is saved to your dashboard automatically.

    How ticket priority maps to severity

    Every scenario here is a real row from the Technical Support ticket tracker. The tracker records High / Medium / Low; L1 works in P1–P4. This is the rule used throughout:

    P1Tracker HighOutage, severe degradation, or data-protection risk. Immediate triage, continuous ownership.
    P2Tracker Medium — unplannedSomething is already degraded: a blocked user, a capacity risk, a reduced resilience margin.
    P3Tracker Medium — plannedApproved change inside a maintenance window, with a rollback option.
    P4Tracker LowRoutine request, lifecycle work, or an alert that already recovered on its own.

    SLA figures shown on each scenario are the tracker’s estimated resolution hours. They are planning estimates, not contractual commitments — your organisation’s approved impact-and-urgency matrix always takes precedence.
